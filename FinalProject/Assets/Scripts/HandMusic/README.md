# Hand Music

Entry: ClinicalMenu → Hand Music → Open front camera. The standalone HandMusic scene has no ARSession and uses an ordinary front-camera WebCamTexture.

Hemispatial neglect design. Everything follows the training side in Settings (`GameSettings.trainLeftSide`), so left and right neglect are handled the same way: the trained-side hand plays; the camera view, targets and a steady gold anchor line sit on the trained side, while instructions, song controls and End session stay on the other side, where they can always be found (the layout is authored for the left and mirrored for the right). Notes are panned toward where they are played. MediaPipe's handedness decides which hand is which, so the other hand never scores; if it reaches a gold circle, a prompt asks for the trained hand instead. On the phone's front camera (whose image the app mirrors like a selfie) MediaPipe reports the patient's left hand as "Right", as seen on a device, so `HandGesture.IsLeftHand` swaps the labels; an unmirrored camera is the reverse.

Modes (Settings → Hand Music):
- Fingers (default): one gold circle at a time on the trained half of the view, with a badge showing the finger count. A gold sweep travels from the other side to it (a lighthouse-style scanning cue) and the circle plays a soft preview of its note. A note plays only when the trained hand holds that many fingers up inside the circle (1–5 for C4–G4; a thumb–index pinch with another finger raised for A4) for the hold time. A wrong count is not played; the guide names the count shown and the one wanted, and a hand picture next to "Next" shows the fingers to raise (white tips, as on the tracked hand). When the count changes, the picture gives a small hop and its fingers rise one by one in counting order.
- Open hand (a separate exercise for a weak hand): a two-step calibration measures that hand closed and opened as far as is comfortable. Each opening past 60% of the patient's own range, after closing below 30%, plays the next note, while the hand is on the trained half. Measure again repeats the calibration.

In Fingers mode the palm centre decides whether the hand is inside, and brief tracking drop-outs are forgiven. Circles move toward the edge after two quick reaches (under 4 s) and back in after a slow one (over 10 s); the farthest reach is saved. The circle can be resized from 50% to 100% of its original size, in Settings or with Smaller/Bigger during play; it starts at 80%.

Sessions are timed (Settings → Hand Music duration, 30 s–10 min). The clock starts when the trained hand first appears (after calibration in Open hand mode) and pauses while a song is being chosen or tracking is lost. Songs wait for the correct note, then repeat until the time is up. When time runs out, or End session is pressed after playing, the shared results screen shows notes played, 1–3 stars, personal bests per mode and length, and a comparison with the last Hand Music session. Stars are calibrated so a person with neglect can earn 3 at the default settings (16 notes in 2 minutes in Fingers mode, 20 in Open hand); a longer hold or smaller circle lowers the target and easier settings raise it (`SessionRewards.HandMusicPace`). Targets always use the planned session length, so ending early never makes stars easier, and a session with no notes earns no stars and no congratulations. Settings shows the current targets. Days in a row with a finished, scoring session (either exercise) build a streak, shown on Home, Progress and the results screen. Results are saved to ProgressStore with the mode and side and appear on Home and Progress. The hold time (0.3–1.5 s) is set in Settings or with Slower/Faster, and is saved.

Tracking: landmark distances are normalized to palm width; openness is measured against each finger's own length. These are initial heuristic thresholds and require testing with intended users; they do not measure clinical ability. CPU inference is capped at 10 frames per second with up to two hands, using a requested 640×480 camera stream at 30 fps. The preview shows the camera's own texture at that rate (turned and mirrored in the UI to match the tracked image), the drawn hand glides between tracking samples, and Hand Music runs at 60 fps. Camera frames remain local and are not saved or uploaded. The synthesized notes do not require microphone permission. Choose song offers Hot Cross Buns and Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star. Full camera expands the preview (preserving its aspect ratio) and keeps a compact guide on the non-trained half.

Dependency: MediaPipe Unity Plugin 0.16.3 (MIT), https://github.com/homuler/MediaPipeUnityPlugin/releases/tag/v0.16.3 . Hand-landmark model: https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/hand_landmarker/hand_landmarker/float16/1/hand_landmarker.task . Model API documentation: https://ai.google.dev/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/hand_landmarker .

Editor smoke check: Unity -batchmode -quit -projectPath <project> -executeMethod HandMusicCheck.Run [-handMusicPreview <folder>] . It checks the gesture gates, synthetic poses, target placement and calibration, plays every mode for both training sides through the real controller (including the results screen and saved session), checks the mirrored layout, that the live preview matches the tracked image for every camera rotation and flip, and that the guide hand raises the same fingers as before and mirrors exactly, and runs native-model inference on a blank frame; the optional folder receives screenshots, including every guide-hand pose and a finger change part-way through. Front-camera orientation, performance and recognition of a real (and weak) hand still require a phone test.
